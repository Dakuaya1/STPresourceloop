02 / THE DEVELOPER CASE

Turn a running cost
into a lasting resource.

An STP earns nothing back. Sludge has to be treated and transported, month after month, and organic waste leaves the site the same way.

The proposal is to add an engineered digestion and gas-use system to the STP the project already has, so that sludge and organic waste become feed instead of a disposal bill. For a new project, the additional cost can be considered during utility planning and overall development budgeting. For an occupied community, the decision rests on measured waste supply, space and the operating case.

The value comes from usable energy and managed outputs. The feasibility study needs to establish how much of that value remains after collection, operation, maintenance and the recovery system’s own electricity demand.

01 / AVOIDED COST

02 / MORE FEED, MORE GAS

Use every organic stream

Food waste, leaves and garden waste go into the digester or the composter, which raises the output and keeps the waste on site.

04 / FOR THE LIFE OF THE BUILDING

A feed that never stops

As long as people live there, they produce sewage and kitchen waste every day. The system’s fuel is built in.

Model assumptions & cost boundaries +
  • 4.5 people per home (editable above); 100 homes per acre for garden-waste scaling.
  • 100 litres of sewage per person per day; STP capacity is population × 100 L/day, converted to MLD.
  • Sludge biogas: 9 L/person/day theoretical yield × 55% capture efficiency. Food waste: 0.15 kg/person/day × 0.12 m³ biogas/kg.
  • Energy: 6 kWh per m³ biogas × 32% generator electrical efficiency. It is a gross planning estimate before the system’s own power use and downtime.
  • Digester reference volume: 50 m³ per MLD in the workbook, not an engineering sizing basis. Validate retention time and loading against the actual feed. Garden waste: 5 kg/acre/day; compost yield assumption: 35% (not used as fertilizer revenue).
  • Core capex: ₹40 / ₹55 / ₹70 lakh per MLD for low / central / high, scaled by max(0.55, (MLD ÷ 1.5)−0.25). The model’s 1.5 MLD reference is embedded in that scale factor.
  • Food-waste preparation equipment: ₹3,000 per kg/day capacity; composting equipment: ₹2,000 per kg/day. The calculator uses ±30% around the combined add-on for low/high totals.
  • Modeled annual value: electricity valued at ₹8/kWh, plus avoided disposal cost and ₹0.43 lakh/MLD/year fertilizer value. Avoided disposal cost is your entered monthly spend × 12; if left blank, the workbook’s ₹0.67 lakh/MLD/year sludge-disposal saving is used. It assumes on-site processing replaces that spend; some digestate handling may remain. These are planning assumptions, not guaranteed cash receipts.
  • Feed scenarios: “sludge only” excludes food-waste and composting equipment and food-waste biogas. Garden waste and leaves are composted; compost sales are not valued.
  • All project cost totals include modeled core capex plus the food-waste and composting add-on. The range is a scale-based estimate, not a design-specific quotation. No O&M, financing, taxes, permitting, land, civil variations, replacement costs or carbon credits are included.

08 / PRACTICAL QUESTIONS

Before you commit.

Where does the saving come from?

Mainly two places. First, sludge that is treated and transported off site today is digested on site, which cuts that recurring bill. Second, the biogas produced, mostly from food waste, runs a generator that offsets purchased electricity. Enter your current disposal spend in the cost planner to see your own figure.

Why add food waste, leaves and garden waste?

STP sludge alone gives a modest amount of gas. Segregated food waste is a much richer feed and raises biogas output several times over, while leaves and garden waste can be composted in the same loop. Residents produce this waste every day for as long as they live there, so the feed lasts the life of the building.

Does this replace the STP?

No. The STP continues treating sewage. The proposed biodigester processes suitable sludge from that system, with food waste added where feasible. Returned liquids and their treatment load must be included in the design.

Is the space already available?

The project already has sewage infrastructure and may have utility space that can be used. A survey must confirm room for the digester, gas equipment, waste handling, access and digestate processing. Additional space or civil works may be necessary.

How much electricity can it produce?

Output depends on occupied homes, actual sludge and food-waste quantities, methane yield and operating uptime. A feasibility study should establish net usable electricity after the system’s own consumption. No fixed generation or payback is promised at concept stage.

Can the remaining material go straight to gardens?

Not automatically. Digestate requires appropriate treatment and testing before use. The design must account for both liquids and solids, compost curing where needed, and the suitability of the finished product.

Who operates the system after handover?

A trained operator and maintenance arrangement are essential. The handover plan should assign waste collection, equipment upkeep, gas safety, output monitoring and operating costs to clearly identified parties.

What is needed to assess a project?

The STP process and capacity, actual occupancy and flows, sludge characteristics, segregated food-waste quantities, a utility-area layout, electricity bills and current waste-management costs. These form the starting point for a site-specific proposal.
